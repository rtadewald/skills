# The five categories

Every element of the screen goes into exactly one category. The question that decides it: **where does the element's
shape come from?**

| # | Category | The shape comes from | Typical elements |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Media** | ready-made pixels: a crop, a generated image, a looping video | textures, a still backdrop |
| 2 | **DOM** | layout and vector: HTML, CSS, SVG, JS (GSAP for motion) | text, cards, charts, icons, HUD rings, connectors |
| 3 | **Particles** | many points with position and motion (Canvas 2D or Three.js) | dust, sparks, data specks, swarms |
| 4 | **Shader** | one formula per pixel | smoke, living backgrounds, glow, distortion, bloom |
| 5 | **3D model** | a mesh (GLB) | a character, a product, an object that turns |

Inside a category, use the **lightest technique that stays faithful**: Canvas 2D before WebGL, CSS before SVG
filters, an image before a shader when nothing moves.

**Not categories, but tools that cross them:**

- **GSAP** animates any property: CSS, SVG attributes, shader uniforms, a bone's rotation.
- **WebGL** is the engine under Shader, 3D particles and 3D models. Three.js is a library on top of it.
- **Cursor, noise and bloom** appear in several categories.

**Edge cases:**

- Particles simulated on the GPU use shaders internally but stay in **Particles**: what you see is a swarm.
- Bloom and other post-processing are **Shader**: a pass over the whole screen.

**Every WebGL element is a `<canvas>`, so it is a layer.** Decide its z-order in the plan. Share one Three.js
renderer when several WebGL elements sit on the same layer.

## DOM

- **Measure, don't guess.** Take positions from the image with image analysis:
  - an arc or ring: collect its bright points and fit center and radius by least squares;
  - nodes and icons: find the bright blobs and use their centers;
  - text: compare the ink box in the image with the page's, adjust font, size and spacing to ±2 px.
- **Fonts first.** Render the candidate Google Fonts side by side with a crop of the text and pick the closest.
  When no font matches a logo (unusual letter shapes), draw it as an SVG path.
- **Repeated items** (nodes on an arc, list rows): keep their coordinates in one place, generated or in a single list.
- **Connectors and travelling light:** at rest the line is faint; the light "fills" the line (`stroke-dasharray`
  animated), stays lit ~1.5 s and fades; the target reacts when the light arrives (a ring that flares).
- **Rotating HUD rings:** concentric SVG circles with dash patterns, each rotated by GSAP at its own slow speed and
  direction (`rotation: 360, svgOrigin: '0 0', repeat: -1, ease: 'none'`).

## Particles

- Canvas 2D for up to a few thousand points in 2D. `THREE.Points` with a custom shader for more, or for depth.
- Cursor interaction: displacement that is **zero at the cursor center and at the edge** (e.g. `x·(1−x)²`), or swirl
  the points around the cursor. A push that is maximal at the center opens a hard-edged hole.

## Shader

- A full-screen quad with a fragment shader. Smoke and soft backgrounds: fbm noise with domain warping, low contrast,
  slow time.
- Keep it light: if the effect is subtle, render at half resolution and scale the canvas up.

## 3D model

- From an image: generate the model with image-to-3D, rig it, animate it in Three.js. Full recipe:
  [character-3d.md](character-3d.md).
- From scratch (abstract objects: spheres, orbs, knots): build it in Three.js with geometry + shaders.

## Media

- A crop of the image, an image regenerated by an image model, or a short looping video (`<video muted loop>`).
- Use it only for what never reacts to anything.
